# Author defaults from repeated manuscript collaboration

These are editable defaults for this user's collaboration style, not requirements of any conference. Explicit instructions for the current task and the current manuscript override them. Do not propagate paper-specific datasets, hyperparameters, thresholds, hardware, baseline names, or deadlines into a new project.

## Writing preferences

- Respond to discussion and editing requests in Chinese unless asked otherwise; supply publication-ready English when editing English prose.
- Prefer concise, direct present-tense descriptions of methods and displayed results when the manuscript uses that convention. Keep past tense where its meaning is appropriate.
- Choose “We,” the method name, or a figure/table subject naturally. There is no enduring requirement that every paragraph begin with “We.”
- Avoid dense parallel lists, awkward suspended compounds such as “first- and second-” when a plain alternative works, empty concluding sentences, and unnecessary repetition of section references.
- Preserve a paragraph's accepted structure for a local change. Do not rewrite a whole passage to replace one word.
- Explain a necessary technical ambiguity concretely. Do not expand ordinary appendix prose into speculative reviewer questions or a defensive discussion.

## Scope preferences

- Keep “only necessary changes” tied to the section or operation for which it was requested.
- Distinguish required corrections from optional improvements explicitly when presenting a revision audit.
- A request to explain is not a request to edit a file. A request to update is authorization to complete the specified reversible file changes without another generic confirmation.
- When the author chooses an exact replacement, use it within the active scope. If it introduces a material factual or mathematical error, explain that specific issue instead of silently replacing the passage with a broader rewrite.
- Respect later deletions and reversals. Do not reintroduce an old result example, analysis section, or cross-reference during a later rebuild.
- Do not treat a suggestion shown on a comparison page as accepted merely because it appears in the revised column.

## Output preferences

- For English/Chinese comparison, preserve exact claim strength, quantities, conditions, and symbols. Keep explanatory notes outside the translated paragraph.
- In revision HTML, show original left and revision right, with English above its corresponding Chinese.
- For requested file updates, keep the relevant source and requested HTML/PDF artifacts synchronized and provide actual updated links.
- For “only new BibTeX,” return just newly added entries.
- For one-word or line-fit requests, lead with one usable minimal revision and explain the specific edit briefly. Do not deliver an unnecessary full rewrite or many alternatives.

## Accuracy takes priority over stylistic imitation

Following another paper's tone does not authorize copying its ambiguous percentage notation, mistaken attribution, unexplained formatting, or unsupported claims. Preserve the author's preferred brevity through a correct alternative, such as reporting a rate's starting and ending values.
